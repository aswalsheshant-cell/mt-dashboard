# MT Executive Storytelling

Craft leadership narratives that move from data to insight to action. Master the WHAT → WHY →
SO WHAT → WHAT NEXT framework and build compelling QBRs, business updates, and decision memos.

## The Core Arc: WHAT → WHY → SO WHAT → WHAT NEXT

Every executive story follows this four-beat structure. Each beat is non-negotiable:

**WHAT (The Fact)**
- State the metric and the change: "Primary NSV grew 12% YoY to ₹1,205L in FY27."
- Include context: time period, comparison (YoY, MoM, vs plan, vs peer)
- No interpretation yet—just the observable fact
- Example: "Mamaearth doubled market share in Modern Trade from 3.2% to 6.4% in Q1 FY27"

**WHY (The Driver)**
- Answer: "What changed in the business to cause this?"
- Isolate 2–3 primary drivers (not a list of 10)
- Use data to prove causation, not correlation
- Example: "Growth driven by (1) new store openings (+180 locations, +22% distribution), 
  (2) pricing realization in Hair Care (+₹8L NSV, +340 bps GM%), (3) trade spend efficiency 
  (ROI improved 2.1x → 2.7x)"

**SO WHAT (The Implication)**
- Translate metrics to business outcomes: "What does this mean for the business?"
- Address: profitability, competitive position, risk, opportunity
- Make the stake clear (not "sales increased" but "competitive threat reduced" or "profit 
  headroom created")
- Example: "At this velocity, we'll exceed FY27 market share target (8.5%) by Q4, creating 
  ₹180L+ margin buffer for contingency"

**WHAT NEXT (The Action)**
- Propose 1–3 decisions or next steps
- Tie each to the SO WHAT (not random initiatives)
- Include resource requirement and success metric
- Example: "Recommend (1) accelerate Hair Care line extension (decision by Aug 31; +3–5 
  market share points), (2) reallocate 15% trade spend to underperforming East region 
  (trial Sep–Oct; measure via offtake lift)"

## Response Hierarchy by Audience

**C-Suite (CEO, CFO, MD):**
- Lead with a single number (the headline)
- Assume 60-second attention window
- Spotlight one major decision or risk
- Show market context (competitive, regulatory, macro)

**Finance (Controller, FPA):**
- Lead with P&L impact (GM%, Expense ratio, ROI)
- Show reconciliation to SAP/GL numbers
- Highlight variance from plan with root cause
- Provide approval recommendation

**Business Units (Brand Head, Category Manager):**
- Lead with channel performance (offtake, share, margin)
- Show per-chain or per-store drivers
- Provide tactical recommendations (repricing, promo, listing)
- Flag data quality issues that affect reliability

## Slide Deck Templates

### Template 1: Performance Dashboard Slide
```
Headline (5–6 words): "Mamaearth Market Share Doubled in Modern Trade"

Visual (left, 60%):
- KPI card: 6.4% (was 3.2%) | +3.2 pts
- Trend sparkline: Q1 FY26 → Q1 FY27 (showing curve)
- Waterfall: +180 stores | +₹18L NSV (pricing) | +₹22L NSV (mix) = +₹40L total

Narrative (right, 40%):
- Mamaearth grew 25% YoY; market grew 8%
- Three drivers: distribution, pricing, mix
- On track for 8.5% FY27 target
- Risk: Competitor launched in Modern Trade; monitor Q2

Callout box:
✓ Decision: Approve Hair Care line extension (₹5L investment, +₹30L NSV upside)
⚠ Risk: Price realized may not sustain if promotional intensity increases
```

### Template 2: Problem-Diagnosis Slide (For Misses)
```
Headline: "Offtake Declined 8% MoM—Root Cause: Seasonal + Weather"

Visual (left):
- Line chart: Offtake Qty last 6 months (V-shape: decline then recovery trend)
- Waterfall: Seasonal decline (−12 pts) | Weather disruption (−4 pts) | Early 
  festival demand (−2 pts) = −8% net. Rebound signal in week 2 of next month

Narrative (right):
- Feb–Mar typically weak (72% historical decline pattern)
- Delhi-NCR region saw 15% disruption (shipping delays, fleet shortage)
- Early Easter buying pulled some demand forward (recover next month)
- Pipeline signal: Pre-bookings +18% for Q2 (leading indicator of recovery)

Callout box:
✓ No action needed; tracking as expected
📊 Forecast: Offtake +12–15% next month (seasonal normalization)
⚠ Watch: If decline extends beyond typical 2-week window, escalate
```

### Template 3: Executive Summary Memo (300 words max)
```
TO:       CFO, Head of Modern Trade
FROM:     Analytics
DATE:     [Timestamp]
SUBJECT:  FY27 Mid-Year P&L Review: 2 Decisions Required

SITUATION:
Mamaearth P&L tracking at ₹245L gross margin (89% of half-year plan). Growth rate 
12% YoY; FY target 18% at risk unless Q3–Q4 acceleration occurs.

KEY FINDINGS:
1. NSV growth strong: +₹95L YoY (+15%) driven by distribution (+180 stores, +22%) 
   and pricing (+340 bps in Hair Care)
2. Trade spend ROI degrading: 2.7x (down from 3.2x prior FY) due to increased 
   promotional intensity in Modern Trade
3. P&L variance: Expense ratio 18% vs plan 16% (−200 bps). Root: 
   unplanned promotional spend in Q2 (−₹12L margin impact)

BUSINESS IMPACT:
At current pace: ₹430L FY27 margin (vs ₹480L target). Gap: ₹50L (10% miss).
Competitive risk: Competitor market share gained 120 bps in Modern Trade in Q2 
(must counter with tactical support).

RECOMMENDED DECISIONS:
Decision #1: Reduce promotional intensity Q3–Q4 by 15% (save ₹18L margin; accept 
2–3 pt market share decline). Success metric: ROI return to 2.9x+ by Q4.

Decision #2: Accelerate Hair Care line extension launch (₹5L capex; +₹30L NSV 
potential over FY27–FY28). Success metric: 4–6 pt market share gain in Hair.

NEXT STEPS:
- Finance approval required by [date] to implement Decision #1
- Product/Brand sign-off required by [date] for Decision #2
- Weekly tracking via dashboard KPI dashboard (Modern Trade → P&L tab)
```

## Metric Language Guide (What to Say vs. What NOT to Say)

| Metric | ✓ Say This | ✗ Don't Say |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| GM% | "Margin contracted 120 bps due to 15% promotional spend jump" | "Margin fell" (vague) |
| Offtake Qty | "Offtake declined 8% seasonally; rebound expected in Q2 per pre-bookings signal (+18%)" | "Offtake is down" (lacks forecast) |
| Market Share | "We gained 320 bps in Modern Trade while market grew 8% (we grew 12%)" | "We're growing" (no context) |
| Trade Spend ROI | "ROI 2.7x—below our 3.0x hurdle; 150 bps promo mix increased" | "ROI is low" (no baseline) |
| Distribution | "Launched in 180 net new stores (+22% coverage); expect 3–5 week ramp" | "Added distribution" (no specifics) |
| Variance | "P&L miss: ₹50L vs ₹480L target (10.4%); root cause is 200 bps expense ratio vs plan" | "We missed plan" (no diagnosis) |
| Forecast | "On pace for ₹430L (−50L vs target); breakeven requires 18% Q3–Q4 growth per plan" | "Tracking okay" (misleading) |

## QBR Arc (Quarterly Business Review)

**Opening (5 min): State of the Business**
- Headline: one number that changes everything
- 3-slide recap: (1) Overall KPIs (NSV, GM%, Market Share), (2) vs. plan & vs. prior year, 
  (3) Key wins and key risks

**Deep Dive (20 min): Primary Narrative**
- Slide 1: Theme (e.g., "Distribution-Driven Growth Outpaces Margin Pressure")
- Slides 2–4: Evidence (NSV by channel, GM% waterfall, market share vs competitor)
- Slide 5: Leading indicators (pre-bookings, pipeline, new store ramp)

**Problem Diagnosis (10 min): If There's a Miss**
- Slide 1: Miss summary (metric, magnitude, vs. plan)
- Slide 2: Root cause waterfall (decompose into 2–3 drivers)
- Slide 3: Time-bound recovery plan with success metrics

**Forward Plan (10 min): Next Quarter**
- Slide 1: Forecast (NSV, GM%, share) with confidence level (high/medium/low)
- Slide 2: Strategic initiatives (1–3 key bets with resource/timeline)
- Slide 3: Decisions required from stakeholders (approval, investment, trade-off)

**Closing (5 min): Calls to Action**
- Recap 1–2 key decisions
- Show approval path (CFO sign-off, Brand approval, etc.)
- Next review date and success metrics to track

## Pressure Test Your Story

Before you present, ask yourself:

1. **Is the headline true?** (Can Finance verify the number in 5 minutes?)
2. **Does SO WHAT flow from WHY?** (Does the implication follow logically from drivers?)
3. **Are you proposing action or just reporting?** (Every story should end with a decision)
4. **What's the risk you're not saying?** (Competitive, macro, data quality—always name it)
5. **Could a peer disagree?** (If so, show the data that proves your reading, not interpretation)

## Response Format
- Lead with headline (5–6 words, no jargon)
- Show the arc: WHAT (fact) → WHY (driver) → SO WHAT (implication) → WHAT NEXT (action)
- For each driver, cite data + time window
- Close with one decision recommendation
- Flag any assumptions or data quality risks
