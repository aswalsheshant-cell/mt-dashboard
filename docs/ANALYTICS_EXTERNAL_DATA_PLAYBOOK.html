The Unfed Model

Five shared references — a chart-selection poster and two LinkedIn posts on Nielsen metrics and AI workflow — sent me looking for what we do not measure. What I found instead: we already built almost everything those posts describe, and never switched it on. The measures exist in the Power BI model. The folders they read from contain nothing but templates.

The framework is built. The feed is empty.

Why none of it runs

PowerBI/RawDataFolders/Nielsen_Monthly/ and PowerBI/RawDataFolders/TDP_Monthly/ each contain exactly two files: a README and a _TEMPLATE_. Zero real months. Every measure above is wired to a table that has never received a row.

The consequence shows up in the July pack. Page 5 computes "share per distribution point" by hand, from figures lifted out of the June PowerPoints — reinventing the intent of MS vs TDP Index, which was written for exactly this and cannot run. That is not a modelling gap. It is a supply gap.

Worth saying plainly: the posts are a weaker version of what someone here already designed. Growth Driver and TDP Opportunity Quadrant go past anything in them. The useful thing the posts do is point at the two unfed fields whose absence costs us most.

Technique · the missing two thirds

Numeric distribution, and the store-quality index it unlocks

Numeric distribution counts outlets: how many stores stock us, over how many stock the category. Weighted distribution counts value: what share of category sales happens in the stores we are in. We hold the second and have never held the first — and the ratio between them is the interesting number.

share = ND × (WtD ÷ ND) × velocity reach store quality share per point of shelf

What this costs us today

Page 5 says Mamaearth Face Wash holds 89.0% weighted distribution. We cannot currently tell whether that means we are in most stores, or in a small number of very large ones. Those are different businesses with different fixes: the first is a velocity problem, the second is a reach problem dressed up as a good number.

The same question sits under the Shampoo call. We recommended holding pack expansion because 81.5% WtD looked like enough shelf. If ND turned out to be, say, 55%, that recommendation would flip — 81.5% of category value concentrated in 55% of doors means the shelf is narrow and rich, and there is real width still to win.

I am not claiming that is the case. There is no ND data. The point is that a decision already made rests on a term we cannot see.

Technique · price versus demand

Volume share is unfed — but the internal version runs today

Value share and volume share moving apart is a pricing story, not a demand story. Our own skill reference already states the rule. Market Share Volume % already exists as a measure. No Nielsen volume data exists to run it on.

The internal offtake source, though, carries Sales Qty next to NSV and MRP — and it is clean: 24 bad rows in 197,645 for July, 0.01%. That supports the same diagnosis on data we already hold, and nobody has ever run it.

The split nobody had run

May → July: −₹4.15 Cr

Volume −₹5.24 Cr. Price and mix +₹1.09 Cr. July's softness is entirely a volume event — 302,151 fewer units than May, down 13.8% — partly offset by selling what did move at a 3.3% higher average price.

Against April rather than the May peak the picture is calmer: units −3.1%, ASP +4.4%, value +1.1%. May was the outlier month, and July is back near where the quarter started.

value change = base ASP × unit change + current units × ASP change Apr→May +4.52 Cr = +4.13 volume +0.39 price volume-led growth May→Jul −4.15 Cr = −5.24 volume +1.09 price volume-led decline

This qualifies the July story on page 3

Page 3 argues July's conversion collapse is the quarter-opening load repeating April — and on conversion that holds, because the denominator moved. But offtake also fell in units, and the loading story does not explain that. Both are true at once: conversion fell because primary spiked, and consumers bought fewer units. The second is a real demand or availability signal, and the value-only view hides it completely.

Craft · chart form

Two findings in the July pack are drawn in the wrong form

The chart poster is a selection aid, and against it the deck holds up in most places — bars for comparison, lines for time. Two exceptions matter, because in both the form is hiding the finding rather than carrying it.

The load-versus-conversion relationship is a scatter, drawn as a table

Page 3's strongest finding is that July conversion falls monotonically with how far a zone billed above its own Q1 run-rate. That is a correlation between two numeric variables — textbook scatter. As five table rows the reader has to reconstruct the relationship mentally; as a scatter the downward slope is the whole argument, visible in one look.

Primary → gap → offtake is a bridge, drawn as grouped bars

₹47.02 Cr billed becomes ₹33.96 Cr sold through, and ₹13.06 Cr sits in between. That is a waterfall: a start, a decrement, an end. Grouped bars show two totals side by side and make the reader do the subtraction. The waterfall shows the subtraction as the chart.

Worth adopting later — box & whisker

Zone conversion ranges 49.9% to 86.3%. A box plot would show that spread and its outliers honestly in one mark, where six bars invite rank-reading of what is really a distribution.

Worth avoiding — pie, donut, radar

We have concentration facts (98.4% in two brands, 82.4% of NPI in two accounts) that tempt a pie. Two-slice pies say less than a sentence. Radar flatters and compares poorly. The poster lists them; that does not make them right here.

Triage

The five AI hacks, against what we already run

HackVerdictWhy
1 · Read the Nielsen/Kantar PDFs already doing This is how page 5 got its competitor distribution — pulled out of the June packs. The constraint is not reading ability, it is that nobody has supplied a machine-readable month.
2 · Always-on competitor monitoring low fit News feeds tell you a competitor launched. Nielsen tells you whether it took share. For an MT analyst the second is the job; the first is ambient noise with a subscription attached.
3 · Synthesise marketplace reviews worth a trial The one genuinely new capability here. Reviews on the 100 ml Face Wash and 250 ml Shampoo — the two declining packs — could separate "availability gap" from "consumers dislike it", which no internal field can currently answer.
4 · Pre-test creative not our lane Brand and media own this. It does not touch channel analytics.
5 · Raw data → strategy deck already doing, harder We generate the pack from source with a channel reconciliation gate, a geometry check and a tie-assertion on every total. A generic deck tool produces slides that look finished without any of that. The slides were never the hard part.
The pattern across all five

Four of the five address speed of production. Our bottleneck has never been production speed — this pack has been rebuilt several times in a day. It has been whether the numbers mean what they appear to mean: eB2B inside MT zone sales, a netted gap hiding ₹2.06 Cr, a conversion collapse that was a billing rhythm. No tool on that list would have caught any of those.
