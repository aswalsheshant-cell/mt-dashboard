Six Books, Six Blind Spots

Your six-book list is sound. But a reading list only pays back when it changes what you compute. So this note skips the summaries and does one thing instead: it names the techniques from each book that your July 2026 pack does not yet use, and proves each one against your own numbers — every figure below is derived from the July Chain NPI Recovery deck itself.

Correction — 16 Aug 2026

A channel reconciliation run after this note was written found that eB2B and SIS sales are inside the MT zone rollup: ₹9.06 Cr of non-MT primary in FY27, and the Nykaa (FSN) account — carried through the deck as an MT zone — is classified eB2B. The rupee figures below were computed on the blended base and are therefore overstated pending recut: the ₹5.87 Cr benchmark prize falls to roughly ₹5.4 Cr, and the ₹15.17 Cr gross gap falls by ₹1.7–2.4 Cr.

The techniques are unaffected — and the Kimball section below is the one that caught it. What that section reports as an unexplained ₹2.07 Cr netting anomaly turned out to be an entire mis-channelled account. Exact restatement is blocked on the source workbook; see the issue record in the repo.

Owns · the grain and the join

The Data Warehouse Toolkit

Ralph Kimball

Already doing: you have clean conformed hierarchies — Chain, Zone, State, Category, Sub-Category, Article, Pack — and you document sources on every slide.

Declare the grain once, then never mix bases

Every fact table gets one stated grain, and every percentage on the page must share the same denominator. Two numbers built on different bases can never appear in the same sentence.

Conformed dimensions must survive the join

A chain that exists in offtake but not in primary means the Chain dimension isn't conformed. That's a modelling defect, not a business result — and it has to surface as an exception before anyone reads a growth number off it.

Additive, semi-additive, non-additive

Rupees add. Ratios do not. Conversion % can never be averaged across zones to get a national figure — it must be recomputed from summed numerator and denominator.

The factless fact table

A store × article × month listing table records what should have sold, giving you a real denominator for distribution and OSA. It's the missing table behind every SAH / WD / PDO field your deck says it can't yet populate.

Proof from your July deck

₹15.17 Cr, not ₹13.11 Cr

Your headline flow gap is ₹13.11 Cr. But add up the six zone gaps on slide 4 and you get ₹15.17 Cr. The difference is exactly the ₹2.07 Cr of FSN/Nykaa offtake that carries no mapped primary — it is silently netted off the national gap. So the deck reports a chain-level percentage (90.7% in DMart + Reliance) against a ₹13.11 Cr base while the zone table sums to a different one. Two bases, one page.

The same defect makes Apollo look stable. Its national conversion reads 99.7% — but the zone pages show 148.5% in South-2, 137.7% in Central and 121.1% in East. The national figure is not a performance; it's an artefact of averaging a non-additive ratio until the errors cancel.

zone gaps · 1.77 + 1.61 + 4.96 + 1.98 + 4.28 + 0.57 = 15.17
15.17 − 2.07 (Pan India offtake, no primary) = 13.10 ≈ 13.11 headline

Root cause found, 16 Aug. The reason Pan India carries no primary is that it is not a geography and not Modern Trade. Its FY27 offtake (860.01 L) equals the Nykaa (FSN) account exactly, 1:1 — and Nykaa's primary is classified eB2B. A non-conforming dimension was the visible symptom; an entire mis-channelled account was the disease. This is the case for the technique, not against it: nothing else in the pack pointed at it.

What it's worth ₹2.06 Cr of recoverable gap is currently invisible — netted away by a dimension that doesn't conform. Report gross positive gap and gross negative gap as two separate lines and both problems become actionable instead of cancelling each other. Following that single thread is what surfaced ₹9.06 Cr of non-MT primary sitting inside zone sales.

Owns · the mechanics that catch it

Python for Data Analysis

Wes McKinney

Already doing: you compute contribution %, rank within group, and month-on-month change — the core groupby vocabulary is clearly in place.

Validated merges

merge(..., how="outer", indicator=True, validate="1:1") is the single highest-return line you are not writing. It tells you which keys exist on only one side and refuses to run if the grain is wrong. Every mapping defect below would have been caught by it automatically.

groupby().transform() for share-of-parent

Contribution, within-zone rank and index-vs-zone-average in one pass, at every level, without a single manual denominator. Wrong denominators stop being possible.

Tidy long form as the working grain

Melt everything to one row per chain × zone × article × month × measure. That single table is what makes the weekly chain × zone × EAN exception review your deck promises actually runnable — right now it's promised on slide 1 and never shown.

Rolling windows and qcut bands

rolling(3).mean() for a stable baseline instead of a single volatile prior month; qcut to cut conversion into Scale / Fix / Hold bands by rule, so the banding is reproducible next month rather than re-judged by eye.

Owns · whether the number means anything

Naked Statistics

Charles Wheelan

Already doing: you correctly flag conversion above 100% as a timing or stock signal rather than a success — that instinct is exactly right.

A materiality floor before any percentage

No growth rate gets shown, ranked or coloured unless the base clears a stated rupee threshold. Below the floor, report the absolute change only.

Regression to the mean

A single extreme month partly reverts on its own. Compare against a rolling 3-month base, not against one prior month, or you will keep launching recovery programmes against noise and then claim credit when it self-corrects.

Simpson's paradox

National conversion can fall while every single zone improves, purely from mix shifting toward weaker zones. Nothing in the current pack can tell those two worlds apart — and they demand opposite actions.

Denominator hygiene

Before interpreting any ratio, ask what is in the bottom. Your chain scorecard covers ₹26.90 Cr of a ₹36.10 Cr national base — 74.5%. That coverage figure belongs on the slide.

Owns · which metric you steer by

Lean Analytics

Alistair Croll & Benjamin Yoskovitz

Already doing: flow conversion % is a genuinely good rate metric, and you already publish it by zone and by chain.

One Metric That Matters

Your weekly scoreboard carries six KPIs of equal weight. Pick one the business steers by this quarter — flow conversion is the right candidate — and demote the rest to supporting. A scoreboard where everything is a priority sets no priority.

Rates over counts

₹36.1 Cr is a count; it only grows. ₹ per store per month, share per distribution point, and conversion % are rates — they can fall while the total rises, which is exactly the warning you want to receive early.

Segment before you average

An average is a hypothesis about homogeneity. Test it: if the spread inside a segment is wider than the gap between segments, the average is meaningless and the segmentation is wrong.

Leading vs lagging

Everything in the pack is lagging — July is already billed and sold. Active EAN count and hero-SKU OSA are the leading pair; put them in front of the outcome, not in the appendix.

The national conversion headline sits between East at 45.3% and South-1 at 83.6% — a 38-point spread. No zone is near the average; it is a number that exists only on the cover slide. Owns · what to do first

Data Science for Business

Foster Provost & Tom Fawcett

Already doing: your Scale / Fix / Hold framing is a decision rule, not just a description — that is the right shape for an analysis to end in.

Expected value ranking

Every proposed action carries ₹ at stake × probability of success × cost to act. Rank by the product. Your 90-day plan lists twelve actions in three time buckets with no rupee value on any of them, so nothing tells the reader which one to start Monday.

Always state the baseline

What does doing nothing deliver? Without that counterfactual no intervention can be credited or killed. There is no "do nothing" line anywhere in the pack.

Lift and targeting

"Fix North and East" is a territory, not a target list. Score every store × EAN cell by expected recovery, sort descending, and hand the field the top decile. Same effort, several times the return.

Look-alike scoring

Apollo converts near parity. Instead of admiring it, extract what makes an Apollo store an Apollo store — order cadence, assortment depth, replenishment frequency — and score every other store for similarity. That produces a ranked intervention list from data you already hold.

Owns · whether anyone acts on it

Storytelling with Data

Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic

Already doing: your slide titles state findings rather than topics — "Two high-conversion zones can fund focused intervention in North and East" is a real sentence with a point. Most decks never get this far.

Cut to the one number that changes a decision

Every element on a slide either supports the decision or competes with it. Thirteen rows where one row carries the story is thirteen chances to miss it.

Preattentive attributes carry the eye

Colour should mark the exception, not decorate the furniture. When every card has a coloured header, colour has stopped carrying information and nothing on the page tells the reader where to look first.

Never restate what the chart already said

Text next to a number should explain why, never repeat what.

Close with the ask

A finding without a named owner and a date is a fact, not a recommendation. Your EVIDENCE / IMPLICATION / ACTION / OWNER footer is the right frame — it just isn't filled in on any slide.
